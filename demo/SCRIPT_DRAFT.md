# Demo script — draft v1 (for Sam's approval before any audio is generated)

Target length ~2:30–2:50 at ~140 words per minute (this draft: about 340 words). Voice: "daniel". Screen: the live Space.
Numbers marked [*] are filled in from the final recorded runs at freeze.

---

**0:00 — The problem**
Scientific papers publish motion numbers: how fast an insect jumps, how high, at what angle. The videos and data
behind those numbers are often published too. Almost nobody checks one against the other.

**0:15 — What Pixels to Proof does**
Give it what the journal published: the PDF, the videos, the tables, the authors' scripts. Nothing else.
Muse Spark reads everything and proposes an audit plan. Code checks every field of that plan against the files.
SAM 3.1 tracks the animal. Physics and arithmetic decide. No number in a verdict is written by a language model.

**0:40 — The agent at work (ledger view)**
Every model proposal is a line in the ledger, next to the check that accepted or rejected it.
Here a quote does not occur in the paper — rejected; Muse Spark is asked again with the reason, and only that field
is replaced. And when we planted a box on the background, the track was rejected and Muse Spark proposed a new one.

**1:05 — Paper one: larval beetles (PLOS ONE)**
[*] claims are recomputed from the paper's own table: [*] reproduce the printed digits, two are reproduced by a
different column than the sentence suggests. Eleven video shots are refused before any tracking, each with a reason.
One jump is measured. Its row in the table is found blind — from geometry only — and the published speed agrees
with ours: REPRODUCED, with the range of values we would have accepted printed beside it.

**1:40 — Paper two: peacock spiders (Journal of Experimental Biology)**
A different journal, a different setup. The video joins recordings with dissolves; Muse Spark splits them from a
contact sheet, and code checks the split. The caption says "playback four times faster"; the frame clock is
adjusted, and gravity is the independent test. The scale is a four-centimetre gap to a vertical target, found on
the image by code. [*] The verdict, and its reason.

**2:15 — Can it be fooled?**
We corrupted the model's answers on purpose — wrong units, wrong frame rates, misread numbers, a box on the
background — twelve kinds. [*] were caught, [*] recovered on their own, and no corruption produced a wrong verdict.

**2:35 — Close**
Muse Spark proposes. SAM 3.1 tracks. Code checks every step and writes it down.
Pixels to Proof: a second reader for the numbers in science.
